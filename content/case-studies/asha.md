## The problem

A non-profit wanted to help rural women start their own businesses, but it had no delivery plan, relied on paper and manual admin, and worked with many local partners on inconsistent terms.

## What I did

I led the end-to-end launch of a rural entrepreneurship programme across eight communities, and rolled out simple digital tools to run it.

## How I did it

- Built a milestone-based delivery plan and RAID log, reporting monthly to trustees
- Managed the programme budget against funder milestones
- Coordinated local partners and suppliers through fortnightly reviews, and renegotiated contracts
- Redesigned workflows end to end and built leadership dashboards to replace manual reporting
- Wrote the business case that secured further investment

## Trade-offs

- **Simple over feature-rich.** Chose basic tools that first-time users could pick up quickly, because adoption mattered more than capability.
- **Standardise before scaling.** Fixed core processes before expanding to more communities.

## The result

The programme supported 250+ women, and the technology rollout reached 95% user adoption within one quarter. Workflow changes cut manual admin by 35%.

*What I'd do differently: instrument feedback loops from the start, so we could tell which communities were struggling before the monthly review cycle.*
