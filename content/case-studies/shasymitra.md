## The problem

Smallholder farmers in India relied on unverified crop advice, faced opaque prices, waited too long to get paid, and had little reason to trust a mobile app.

## What I did

I founded and built ShasyMitra, an AI-enabled platform combining crop advisory, payments and a marketplace, backed by physical hubs where farmers could get verified seeds and soil tests.

## How I did it

- Spent time in the field with farmers before deciding what to build
- Launched free advisory first, and introduced payments and the marketplace only once farmers trusted the product
- Built AI crop-disease diagnosis that sends unclear cases to an agronomist instead of guessing
- Designed the app to work offline, and integrated payments with automated reconciliation to speed up settlement

## Trade-offs

- **Trust before revenue.** Delayed monetisation, because asking for money before proving value would have lost farmers.
- **Accuracy over speed.** Accepted slower answers on unclear AI cases, because one wrong diagnosis can ruin a harvest.
- **Narrow over broad.** Launched with only the most common regional crop diseases rather than a national catalogue.

## The result

ShasyMitra reached 50,000+ farmers and £870K+ in annual recurring revenue, and cut the payment settlement cycle to 4 days.

*What I'd do differently: build product analytics in from day one, and bring on more suppliers per category earlier.*
